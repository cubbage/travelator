Get crisp US $100 bills in Yokohama

Start with Travelex at Yokohama Porta: its store page says it sells US dollars and accepts credit cards for foreign currency purchases. Call before going to confirm your card, the number of $100 notes, and their condition.

Travelex’s official store page lists USD among its currencies and says customers can buy foreign currency with a credit card. It does not promise a particular denomination, crispness, or stock level. Its FAQ says to call the chosen branch about denomination requests; requests depend on stock. Porta store details · Travelex FAQ.

Confirm these four things on the call

  1. Do you have enough US $100 bills for the amount I need on my visit date?
  2. Can you accept my US-issued credit/debit card for this in-store currency purchase?
  3. Can you select clean, flat notes with no folds, stains, tears, stamps, or writing?
  4. Can you reserve the notes, and may I inspect them before the card is charged?
Reduce the trip burden: If walking is difficult, ask the hotel front desk or a trusted companion to make this call, confirm a reservation, and help arrange a taxi. Before setting out, ask the hotel and shop to confirm the closest drop-off and a step-free route; do not assume station or mall access is step-free.

Stock and note condition are not guaranteed by the store webpage. Ask for a reservation by name and date if the branch offers one.

A short Japanese request

Show or read this when calling. Replace the bracketed amount with your target.

米ドルの100ドル札を、合計［金額］ドル分探しています。

折り目や汚れ、書き込みのない、状態の良い紙幣はありますか。

海外発行の［クレジット／デビット］カードで支払えますか。

在庫を取り置きできますか。支払い前に紙幣を確認できますか。

“I’m looking for US $100 bills totaling [amount]. Do you have bills in good condition without folds, stains, or writing? Can I pay with a foreign-issued [credit/debit] card? Can you hold the notes, and may I inspect them before paying?”

If the branch cannot confirm the denomination, card type, or condition, call again later or ask whether it can direct you to another nearby branch. Do not make a special trip based only on general USD availability.

Keep the card cost predictable
Before you go: ask the card issuer how a foreign-currency purchase at a Japanese exchange shop will post. Confirm foreign transaction fees and, for a credit card, whether this merchant transaction could be treated as a cash advance, with an advance fee or immediate interest.
  • For a debit card, ask the shop whether it accepts foreign-issued debit cards; the public store listing only confirms credit-card purchases.
  • For a credit card, check the issuer’s terms and available cash-advance limit before using it.
  • Ask the counter for the final JPY charge and compare the displayed rate with another option before paying.

The plan here assumes payment by card and no reimbursement for foreign ATM fees, so it does not route the purchase through an ATM. Withdrawing yen first can add an unreimbursed ATM fee and a separate exchange spread.

Inspect and protect the bills

If these dollars are intended for Myanmar, strict note condition matters. The UK travel advice says exchange counters check notes carefully, prefer high denominations, and may reject notes with pen marks, folds, or tears; some may refuse serial numbers beginning AB or CB. The U.S. Commerce Department also advises new notes without marks or creases. GOV.UK cash guidance · U.S. Commerce country guide.

  • Before leaving the counter, check each note under good light for folds, creases, stains, tears, stamps, and writing.
  • Ask for replacements before completing the purchase if any note is not acceptable to you.
  • Check serial prefixes if applicable to your destination’s exchange rules.
  • Carry notes flat in a paper envelope or other rigid protection; avoid folding them into a wallet.

“Crisp” is a request, not a grading standard. The destination exchanger makes the final acceptance decision.

If the card-funded shop cannot help

World Currency Shop (WCS) lets customers request denominations, but its official FAQ says it does not accept credit cards, and its service page says shop counters exchange yen cash for foreign currency. It is not a fit for this card-funded plan unless you already have yen cash available. WCS FAQ · WCS exchange steps.

Do not withdraw yen solely to use WCS without first comparing the full cost: ATM fee, your bank’s foreign transaction charge, and WCS’s quoted exchange rate. With no ATM-fee reimbursement, that route can cost more than the card purchase.
